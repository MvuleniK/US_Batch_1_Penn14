FAQ topics

When should I schedule my child's first visit?

As soon as your child gets their first tooth. The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends a visit by six months after the first tooth erupts, or at age one, whichever comes first.

How is a pediatric dentist different from other dentists?

Pediatric dentists complete dental school plus several years of specialized training in treating infants, children and adolescents, with expertise in childhood development and behavior. The office design and team are geared to young visitors.

What happens at my child's first visit?

Usually short and simple: getting to know your child, checking teeth, gums and jaw, a little cleaning if needed, and answering your questions about caring for your child's developing teeth.

How can I prepare my child?

Keep a positive attitude, because children pick up on adults' apprehension. Look at the pictures of the office and team together, and explain that Dr. Abbey will help keep their teeth and gums healthy.

How often should my child visit?

Generally every six months, with more frequent visits if your child's oral health calls for it.

Why do baby teeth need special care?

Baby teeth help children speak, smile and chew properly, and they hold space in the jaw for permanent teeth. A child's general health is also affected by the health of their teeth and gums.

When should my child have dental X-rays?

The practice generally recommends a first set around age four, and regular (at least yearly) X-rays once baby teeth in the back are touching. Children at higher risk may need them earlier.

Does my child need sealants, or a mouthguard for sports?

Sealants cover hard-to-brush pits and fissures, especially on molars. For children active in sports, the practice recommends mouthguards and can discuss a custom-fitted one.

What is orthodontics?

A specialized form of dentistry, also called dentofacial orthopedics, focused on the diagnosis, prevention and treatment of dental and facial abnormalities.

Who is an orthodontist?

A dental specialist with two to three years of additional training and experience beyond dental school.

What's the best age to visit the orthodontist?

Any age can be a great time. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends children first visit around age seven, and about one in five orthodontic patients is over 21.

Do braces hurt?

Braces do not often hurt, though you may feel a small amount of discomfort for a couple of days as your mouth gets used to them.

How do I take care of my teeth with braces or a retainer?

Brush after meals, floss daily, use fluoride toothpaste, avoid sticky, chewy and hard foods, and keep your retainer clean and in its case. Keep seeing your dentist every six months.

Will braces interfere with sports, instruments or singing?

You may need a short adjustment period, but braces won't stop you taking part. For contact sports, wear a mouthguard to protect your braces.

What about costs?

Cost varies with the treatment plan, and the practice offers various finance plans. No prices are quoted here.

How do I schedule my next appointment?

Call the office. New patients can send an appointment request and the scheduling coordinator will follow up.
