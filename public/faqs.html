Pediatric dentistry questions

Four short answers from Dr. Abbey's side of the practice, for parents of infants through teens.

Read all pediatric FAQs

When should my child first see a dentist?

As soon as the first tooth comes in. The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry advises a visit within six months of the first tooth, or by age one, whichever comes first.

What happens at the first visit?

It is usually short and simple. The team gets to know your child, checks teeth, gums and jaw, cleans a little if needed, and answers your questions.

Why do baby teeth need a pediatric dentist?

They help children speak, smile and chew, and they hold space in the jaw for permanent teeth.

Does my child need sealants from a pediatric dentist?

Sealants cover hard-to-brush pits on molars. For sports, the practice also recommends mouthguards and can discuss a custom-fitted one.

Orthodontics questions

Four short answers from Dr. Jake's side of the practice, for children, teens and adults.

Read all orthodontic FAQs

What is the best age to see an orthodontist?

Any age can be a good time. The AAO recommends a first visit around age seven, and about one in five orthodontic patients is over 21.

Do orthodontic braces hurt?

Braces do not often hurt, though your mouth may feel some discomfort for a couple of days as it adjusts.

What about costs?

Cost varies with the treatment plan, and the practice offers finance plans.

How do I schedule my next orthodontic visit?

Call the office. New patients can send an appointment request and the coordinator will follow up.
